This page turns a product reference into a visual prompt for planning new campaign scenes. It focuses on what shoppers need to recognize—shape, materials, packaging and label placement—then separates those details from the setting you want to change. One product photo, two campaign outputs

Illustrative planning example: a front-facing amber skincare bottle on a plain background. The prompts below are hand-written examples of how to separate product facts from a new scene; they are not claimed as tool output.

  1. Record the product facts

    Amber glass dropper bottle, black cap, white rectangular label, centered logo and small volume text. Verify each visible detail against the actual photo. Do not invent ingredients or performance claims.

  2. Write the scene image prompt

    “Photograph the same amber glass dropper bottle on a pale travertine shelf beside soft linen. Keep its bottle silhouette, black cap, white label layout, logo position and readable proportions. Morning window light, restrained shadows, warm neutral palette, editorial product photography.”

  3. Draft ad copy separately

    Use the product and scene brief to write a headline and caption yourself. The image tool provides a visual prompt; it does not generate or verify advertising claims.

Before you publish a generated product scene

Inspect packaging

Compare cap shape, label layout, logo and printed text against the original photo. Image models may distort them.

Control the setting

Specify surface, props, lighting and camera crop without quietly changing the product itself.

Check the offer

Keep pricing, availability, ingredients and performance claims outside the visual prompt unless they have been verified.

Product image to prompt FAQ

Will the tool create advertising copy?

No. It creates an image generation prompt from the uploaded picture. The editable brief on this page helps you draft copy manually.

Can it preserve exact packaging and label text?

A prompt can call out the visible details, but image generation may change typography or geometry. Compare each output with the original product and retouch or use a product reference when accuracy matters.

What image should I upload?

Use a sharp product photo with the item unobstructed. A clean view makes shape, material, color and packaging details easier to describe.
