Describe the character in two layers

Consistency improves when the prompt makes a hard distinction between the details that define the character and the details that define this particular shot.

Identity anchor

Record face shape, hair silhouette, eye color, skin tone, distinctive marks, body proportions, and signature accessories from the reference image.

What stays fixed

Repeat the identity anchor when you change the scene. Keep the wording concrete instead of relying on a vague name or style label.

What changes

Put pose, expression, clothing, camera angle, action, lighting, and environment in a separate edit instruction.

A prompt cannot guarantee an identical face in every output. Use a reference image and the identity-control features of your chosen model or workflow when exact continuity matters.

Reference evidence for a recurring character

The useful part of an image-to-prompt result is not a fictional character name. It is a precise record of visible anchors that another image workflow can reuse.

Portrait anchor

Keep the identity fixed while changing only the portrait treatment.

Identity anchor: young shrine guardian with a heart-shaped face, straight black bob with a loose strand over the right cheek, warm brown eyes, small beauty mark below the left eye, red scarf, white fox mask with a blue cord.

What stays fixed: face shape, bob silhouette, eye color, beauty mark, red scarf, fox mask design.

What changes: three-quarter portrait, calm expression, soft winter window light, muted blue background, shoulders-up framing.

Action variation

Preserve the same character while changing pose and camera energy.

Identity anchor: same shrine guardian, black bob, warm brown eyes, beauty mark below the left eye, red scarf, white fox mask tied at the hip.

What stays fixed: facial features, hair silhouette, body proportions, scarf color, mask shape.

What changes: running through falling snow beside a vermilion torii gate, one hand lifting the scarf, low camera angle, determined expression, blue twilight, dynamic motion in the robe hem.

Scene and outfit variation

Change the world around the character without losing the visual anchor.

Identity anchor: recurring young shrine guardian with a heart-shaped face, straight black bob, warm brown eyes, left-cheek beauty mark, and white fox mask.

What stays fixed: face, hair, eyes, beauty mark, petite proportions, recognizable fox mask.

What changes: charcoal travel coat over the red scarf, lantern-lit mountain train platform at night, light rain, 50mm medium shot, quiet watchful expression, amber and teal palette.

How to use the result

  • Start from a clean reference. Use a clear face and enough of the body or signature accessories to make the identity anchor observable.
  • Write fixed details once. Keep the identity block stable and put every new pose, outfit, or setting in the change block.
  • Add workflow controls. Use the reference-image, character-reference, IP-Adapter, ControlNet, or LoRA feature available in your chosen model when the face must stay close.

Character consistency FAQ

Can a prompt guarantee the exact same character?

No. The prompt describes visible identity evidence, but exact continuity also depends on the reference image, model, seed, character-reference features, LoRA, or other workflow controls.

What should stay fixed?

Keep face shape, hair silhouette, eye color, distinctive marks, proportions, and signature accessories fixed. Put pose, expression, outfit, camera, and setting under what changes.

Should I use this with Nano Banana or Stable Diffusion?

Both can use the structure as a starting point. Nano Banana-style edits focus on explicit changes and identity preservation; Stable Diffusion workflows may add reference adapters, ControlNet, or a character LoRA.
